Art and art history for homeschoolers

These sessions turn homeschool art from routine lessons into inspired, engaging learning. Students explore great artists, diverse cultures and creative techniques, while developing critical thinking, visual literacy and self-expression.

Each session pairs art history with a practical activity. For example, we might study Monet’s landscapes, then create our own impressionist-inspired artwork.

It’s not just about finishing projects. It’s about nurturing creative, independent thinkers who feel more connected to history, literature and culture.

Online after-school art classes

Classes that combine creativity, learning and fun. Each one opens with an art history topic, like the vibrant patterns of Van Gogh’s landscapes or Frida Kahlo’s bold portraits.

Then it’s hands-on time. Students experiment with drawing, painting or mixed media, developing their skills, learning to express themselves visually and growing in confidence.

School holiday art program

Live, interactive holiday classes, designed to spark curiosity, encourage experimentation and build strong creative foundations.

Students explore drawing, painting and mixed media through guided activities that balance fun with skill development. Alongside the making, they’re introduced to age-appropriate art history and creative thinking tools that help them see the world differently.

Sessions are tailored by age and interest, with options for:

  • Individual classes
  • Sibling sessions
  • Small group learning

Learning online

Why online learning works

  • Screen time becomes creative time

    Classes are about hands-on making, exploring and expressing ideas, not scrolling.

  • Learning from anywhere

    Students learn from home, so classes are open to families anywhere in the world, including remote and regional areas.

  • A familiar space

    Being somewhere comfortable helps many children settle in, so they can focus on learning and creating.

  • No travel

    No commute and no travel costs, so there’s less rushing for the whole family.

  • Flexible scheduling

    Classes are easier to fit around family life, and can carry on during travel or unexpected disruptions.

  • Steady progress

    Regular classes build routine and skills over time, at a pace that suits each student.
